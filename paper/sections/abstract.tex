Video reasoning models increasingly act as general-purpose ``world models'' for downstream tasks such as robotics, simulation validation, and safety monitoring. 
However, existing video benchmarks often conflate temporal reasoning with static shortcuts, provide limited diagnostic structure, or focus on generative plausibility rather than falsifiable physics checking. 
We introduce \textbf{MIRAGE-V}, a taxonomy-driven benchmark for \emph{physics verification in videos} built from \emph{minimal-change paired clips} that differ by a single controlled intervention aligned to a failure category (e.g., temporal aliasing, occlusion identity drift, latent property inference, conservation-law violations). 
Each example includes a \emph{critical time window}, enabling sampling-robust evaluation and optional localization.

We release (i) an open-source generator for scalable pair creation, (ii) a unified evaluation harness supporting both open-weight and API-accessible models, and (iii) analysis protocols with shortcut audits (text-only, single-frame, shuffled frames, and sparse-vs-dense sampling). 
On a \NumExamples-example evaluation across \NumModules modules with \FramesPerVideo uniformly sampled frames per video, the best model achieves \BestAcc\% accuracy but only \BestPairAcc\% pair accuracy, indicating that even frontier models frequently fail to discriminate minimally different clips and to detect subtle physics violations.
